WT Skin Studio

Browser editor for War Thunder user skins. Everything stays on your machine.

Chrome won’t open folders inside Program Files, where Steam installs War Thunder by default. That is a browser rule, not a bug.

Pick the War Thunder folder. The browser may ask to “upload” it — nothing leaves your PC; that is its wording for reading a folder. You pick it again each visit, and export with Download .zip.

Everything runs on your machine. The game folder is only ever read — the one thing written inside it is your skin's UserSkins folder, when you export there.

Workflow
1. On the welcome screen pick "Open War Thunder folder" — Chrome or Edge can open a folder, remember it and write into it; an install under Program Files is opened with "Open it another way…" — then search a vehicle in the sidebar and click it: its model and textures load straight from the game. Or start empty and drop in an .obj / .dds / .wtproj. 2. Pick a texture slot and add layers — + Layer, Img, Text or Group at the top of the Layers panel. 3. Paint in 2D or press B and paint on the model. 4. Export as… ▸ To War Thunder → in-game "Update custom camouflage list". Where the browser cannot write into UserSkins — an install under Program Files — it offers Download .zip instead, which works everywhere.

Where your files go
Inside your War Thunder folder the app writes only UserSkins/<name>/, and only when you press To War Thunder. Everything else — a .wtproj, a .zip, an OBJ, an exported layer, your settings, a session recording — goes wherever the browser saves downloads; reopen a .wtproj with Add files… or by dragging it onto the window. The recovery copy and the index of your install stay in the browser's own storage on this machine. The .zip holds one folder, <name>/: extract it into UserSkins, so that UserSkins/<name>/ holds the .blk — Windows' Extract All proposes a folder named after the zip and nests it one level too deep — and under Program Files, Windows asks for administrator approval to write there. On a later visit a remembered War Thunder folder may need one click on Reopen War Thunder folder.

Reporting a bug
Say which version (on the welcome card), which browser and which vehicle, and what you did. F12 ▸ Console shows any error in red — copy it. For something slow or jumpy, View & scene ▸ Advanced ▸ Record a session, press F9 the moment it goes wrong, then stop and attach the .json it saves. The app sends nothing anywhere, so a report holds exactly what you put in it.

Which in-game vehicle your skin lands on
War Thunder finds a skin by the unit name of the .blk file, and that is usually not the 3D model's name — the F-4E's model is f_4e but the file must be f-4e.blk, and the M1A2 SEPv3's model is m1a2_sep3 while the file must be us_m1a2_sep3_abrams.blk. The app reads the real unit list out of your install and writes the right names for you. When several vehicles share one model (every F-16A, both Leopard 2A4s) it lists them all under Skin & export ▸ Applies to so you can tick exactly the ones you want.

Ground vehicles vs aircraft
Ground and naval vehicles are exported with set_tex + param:t="camo_skin_tex", which is what the CDK requires for a fixed camouflage and what keeps in-game decals working; aircraft use replace_tex and can only be fixed skins.

The albedo's alpha is the coverage mask — this is the one that catches everybody
A ground or naval skin is not painted on its own: the game blends it over the player's scalable camouflage using your sheet's alpha channel. The CDK puts it plainly — a black alpha means the texture is completely transparent and does not affect the camouflage, a white alpha means it covers the camouflage completely, and greys give a semi-transparent overlay. The app keeps that mask as a channel of its own, so painting colour — even flooding a hull with one opaque colour — never touches it. What the export writes into it is Skin & export ▸ Alpha. On a ground or naval skin (set_tex), Auto — opaque for ground ships it fully opaque (255), as every published ground skin does: the game keeps the vehicle's own texture beside your sheet, and the worn edges, scratches and bare metal come from that texture's alpha, scaled by the in-game condition slider — not from yours. Your sheet's mask ships only when you paint one with the Wear mask brush, or set Alpha to Keep the source / painted mask, which keeps the mask the vehicle's texture came with. On an aircraft (replace_tex) the sheet is the albedo, and Auto keeps its mask. The viewport blends with the same setting — what you see is what the exported .dds contains.

Why a stock tank or ship looks rusty-brown
Because that is what its albedo really is. War Thunder paints ground and naval vehicles from a second texture — the camo slot — and the texture you edit is only the dark, detailed base underneath it: camo_hellgrau_50 is light grey, camo_brown_wood is a wooden deck, ger_camo_bronzegruen is Bundeswehr green. One model uses several of them, which is why a ship's hull, deck and waterline are three different colours over the same sheet. View & scene ▸ Stock camo preview reproduces that in the viewport (preview only — never exported), weighted by the coverage mask above, so painting an opaque colour hides it exactly where the game would.

Layers, and limiting one to a few parts
The stack is per texture. Drag layers to reorder, right-click for the layer menu (rename, duplicate, clear, mask, delete), double-click a name to rename, Alt-click an eye to show only that layer, Ctrl+D duplicates and Del deletes.
Any layer can be limited to a set of model parts: select the parts (Parts tab, or Ctrl+click in 3D) and use the layer's Limit ▸ ← selection — or just add the layer while a selection is live and it inherits it. The limit is non-destructive (clear it any time), it names itself after the part group when it matches one, and the brush respects it, so paint cannot escape onto the neighbouring panel. This is the fast way to do "green turret, grey hull" without masking anything by hand.

Painting, and the brush tools
One switch turns painting on and off for both views — the Painting button under the brush, the button over either view, or B. Off, a click in 3D picks parts and orbits and a drag in 2D pans; on, both views paint with the selected tool, and picking up a tool switches painting on by itself. While it is on, a circle is projected on the surface under the cursor showing the brush's real footprint — brush size is set in texture pixels, which covers half a drop tank and a hand's width of fuselage at the same number, so the ring is worked out from the triangle's own texels-per-metre. The inner circle is where a soft brush stops being opaque.
Color/Eraser work on paint layers. Smoothness/Metal/Flatten normal/Panel line edit the _n surface map with correct WT channel packing. Wear mask paints the albedo's alpha — where your paint covers the player's camouflage — on ground and naval vehicles. Mask paints the active layer's mask (paint hides, Alt or value≥128 reveals). Tools that need something you haven't loaded are greyed out with the reason; a tool that writes a channel the selected layer does not carry yet adds that channel to the layer with its first stroke — and only the options a tool actually uses are shown, so a value slider never appears for the colour brush and a colour picker never appears for a metalness layer.
Shift+click draws a straight line from where the last stroke ended, however far away that is. Painting across a UV island border is handled from the geometry rather than guessed at from the texture: each triangle's own texels-per-metre says how far a stamp should have moved on the sheet, so a real seam crossing is recognised and never bridged with a streak through unrelated islands, and the pointer's travel is walked in small steps that are each raycast for their own surface point, so the gap on both sides of the seam is filled instead of skipped.

Layer masks
Any paint, image or fill layer can carry a mask: a sheet whose brightness decides how much of the layer shows — white shows, black hides, grey partly shows — and nothing is deleted, so removing the mask brings the layer back whole. Add one white (then hide where you paint) or black (then reveal where you paint), take one from the parts you have selected, or load an image as the mask — a stencil, a grunge map, a hand-drawn shape. Once it exists you can paint on it with the Mask brush, invert it, soften it into a fade, fill it to show or hide everything, or replace it from another image, all undoable. The mask appears as its own thumbnail beside the layer's content in the stack; click it to start painting on it. A mask works on every channel, not just colour.

Decals & images
Every image you add opens in the decal editor first, showing it exactly as it is: remove its background if you want to (Key starts on "Keep as-is" — corner colour, white/black, or click the colour you mean), crop to content, adjust brightness/contrast/saturation, rotate/mirror, and pick the surface look. The editor generates the matching normal sub-texture, and Save to library keeps the finished decal — image, generated _n and surface look — for any later project (the Img button opens the library once you have one). Drop a file, or copy an image anywhere and press Ctrl+V.
Placing: a new image lands on the model straight away, projected from the current view onto whatever is in the middle of it — so line the camera up the way you want it to read first. It wraps around curvature and crosses UV seams. The switch at the top of its panel, On the model / On the texture, lays it flat on one texture instead (the way the texture is unwrapped) or puts it back on the surface — in place, at the same size, one Ctrl+Z either way. A marking over a wing root covers two textures at once; it needs a layer in each, but it stays one decal: move it, resize it, hide it or delete it and every texture it spans follows. The layer list marks it "×2 tex".
Moving one afterwards: drag it — on the model or on the texture, whichever placement it has; grab it anywhere inside its frame — nudge with ←↑→↓, rotate with , .. It re-projects onto whatever surface it lands on, so it never smears. Size and Rotate re-project from the original image rather than stretching baked pixels, and Re-aim from this view fixes a decal that was projected from the wrong angle. Its pixels are regenerated on every move, so the brush won't touch a projected layer — duplicate it (⧉) first, which frees the pixels for painting. "Move to clicked spot" is flat UV placement, useful for flat panels. Symmetry mirrors the projection across the vehicle centreline.

Parts, groups & selection
The model's own mesh names (elevator, airbrake_l, gear_c…) become individual parts, and the grouping follows the vehicle type: aircraft get wings / engines & nozzles / propellers / landing gear / pylons, tanks get hull / turret / running gear / applique armour & ERA / stowage / optics, ships get main and secondary battery / directors & radar / masts / boats / propulsion. (The rules come from a survey of the naming conventions across WW2 and modern models of every class.) Groups fold (click the arrow) and can be reorganised: select parts, then "→ group…" moves them into any group or a new custom one. There are two ways to limit where paint goes — select the handful of parts you want to paint, or switch the Parts tab to protect mode and click the one or two parts that must stay untouched (much faster than selecting the other 200). Protected parts show a shield and are skipped by painting and projection. Solo isolates parts/groups.

Game files
Filter the vehicle list by type, nation and rank, and all three are multi-select: click Germany and Italy, ranks V and VI, aircraft and helicopters, and you get exactly that set in one gesture. On a window less than about 810 px tall the nation and rank chips fold behind one chip at the end of the type row, showing a flag and Rank: click it, or press Enter or Space on it, to show or hide them; while they are hidden, a nation or rank you picked shows on that chip, lit. Nation and rank come from the game's own files (shop.blk's research tree and wpcost.blk), so a Hungarian Leopard is listed under Italy and an Australian Abrams under Britain, exactly as in game; with nothing typed the list is ordered the way the trees are, by nation then rank then name. The results share the tab's height with your skins: drag the line between the two sections to give one list more (double-click it to put it back), and the share is remembered. On a short window the vehicle list keeps five rows (fewer only on the very shortest pages), and where there is no room for everything the tab scrolls under the search box instead. Opening the folder also lists every skin already in your UserSkins directory: click one to load it onto the right model. Loading a skin resets the previous one first, and keeps the skin's own resolution (an 8K skin stays 8K rather than being squeezed into the stock texture's size).
"Open War Thunder folder" indexes every texture in the game's .dxp.bin packs (read-only). All formats decode — including Oodle-compressed and BC7 — and each texture automatically resolves to the largest quality tier your install has ($hq/$uhq variants; pick Base in the quality dropdown for smaller loads). Search a vehicle name to load its geometry + correct textures directly (the download arrow at a row's right end downloads it as OBJ + MTL + textures in a .zip instead). For single textures use "add" (auto-bind by name) or "→slot" (apply as the active slot's base — that's how you load any stock camo). "Fill missing slots" completes every empty slot in one click.

Limits
A fill on selected parts used to leave pale lines and unpainted patches. Two separate causes, both fixed. A part stencil is a filled triangle mesh, and a filled shape is antialiased, so every pixel an island only half-covered came out at half strength; unioning two neighbouring parts made it worse rather than better (half over half is three quarters, not one). Stencils are now rasterised as a single path for the whole selection and driven to fully opaque, then grown a couple of texels into the padding around each island — without that last step the renderer samples into unpainted bleed and every island gets a dark rim (View & scene ▸ Advanced ▸ Paint bleed if you ever need to change it). The unpainted patches were a different thing: one mesh node often uses several materials, and those usually live on different textures, so a fill made in one slot could not reach the rest. The Parts tab now lists the node, selecting it takes all of its pieces, and a fill / adjustment / paint layer made from a selection is created on every texture that selection covers and stays linked — moving, hiding, recolouring or deleting it reaches all of them at once.

Layers work on every channel, not just colour. + Layer adds a layer on the channel the brush is in, and the selected layer carries a channel strip: five chips — Colour, Smoothness, Metalness, Normal, Paint mask — saying which channels it holds and what is in each. A + chip gives the layer that channel; a chip's ▾ converts, moves or removes it. Blend mode, opacity, masks, part limits and the cross-texture link work the same on all of them, so surface work is no longer a destructive brush on the imported _n. The 3D view's channel dropdown shows any one map flat and unlit, and the Channels switches under the slot list turn a channel off in the shaded view to see what it contributes.

Suspended armament. The Weapons tab reads the vehicle's own file out of aces.vromfs.bin, so it lists the real thing: every hardpoint, with the presets it accepts and the modification each one needs, in a row laid out the way the game's loadout screen lays it — seen from the front, so the right wing is on the left. The hardpoint row is the loadout. Click a hardpoint to see what it can carry — under the game's own headings, each line the store's name with its ammo and mass — pick one, and it hangs on the aircraft there and then, that station alone: no Apply step, the way the game's loadout screen works. The dropdown at the top fills the stations from one of the game's own purchasable loadouts — or from one you saved — and the moment you change a station it is marked edited and becomes yours. Give it a name and it is kept between sessions, and the loadout a skin was painted against is saved inside the .wtproj and hung again when the project is reopened. The line above the row totals what the build weighs, out of the store definitions themselves, against the vehicle's own maximum load. Hanging a station loads each store's exact model — the rack from its container definition, the missile or bomb from the store definition — and hangs it on the actual model node the game hangs it from, so a MER's three bombs sit on its canted shoulder stations rather than being stacked under the nearest pylon-shaped part. The pylons the game shows for that loadout are shown and the rest hidden, exactly as in game. Nothing here is name-matched any more; the fuzzy search is only for the box you type into. A weapon can also be opened on its own to paint and export, and a saved weapon project can be merged into a vehicle project.
A vehicle loads as a bare airframe. There is no question about what to bring with it, because both answers loaded exactly the same geometry and only differed in what was made visible — so it is a display option. No stores are hung, and the vehicle's own pylons, racks and built-in pods are hidden; switch them back on under View & scene. Which nodes count as mounting hardware comes from the game's own WeaponSlots — the emitter node of each station plus the ShowNodes / HideNodes it switches per loadout — rather than from their names, which is why an A-4E now arrives without the gun pods under its belly. The jet-exhaust, muzzle-flash and flare billboards are off by default too (View & scene): they are drawn unlit and additive, so on a static model they read as dark rectangles, and they have no paintable texture of their own.

Only LOD 0 and rigid (non-skinned) meshes are extracted. Moving parts are posable from the vehicle's own baked animation (<model>_anim), so the gear, flaps, slats, airbrake, control surfaces, arrestor hook, swing wings and even the individual exhaust-nozzle petals move exactly as they do in game rather than about a guessed hinge — sliders run 0 (retracted/closed) to 100 (extended/open), which are the game's own frame numbers, and each rests at the frame the model is drawn in. Vehicles the install ships no clip for, and imported .obj files, fall back to a hinge inferred from the shape of the part (dimmed labels in the Moving parts panel); tank turrets, guns and hatches always do, because a tank's clip carries only track roll. Camo nets and tarpaulins are bone-driven cloth: the file stores them in bind-pose bone space, so without skinning they render as a stretched slab — they are hidden by default and listed with a · in the Parts tab (View & scene ▸ Advanced ▸ Show unposed parts brings them back; their textures are editable in 2D regardless). Tank tracks are not in the model file: War Thunder builds the ribbon at runtime from the suspension nodes, so the preview generates one from the wheels (marked "generated") to carry the track texture, and the track material gets its own 2D-only slot that still exports with the skin. Under Skin & export ▸ Textures out each edited texture can be given its own export resolution and format — that is how you keep one 8K albedo while everything else drops to 1K to fit the archive limit. Projects (.wtproj) embed the model + textures, so one file restores everything.
